No source of truth

Two systems, and nobody could say which one was right

Call notes and client history sitting in one tool, deals and activities in another — both tracking the same thing, neither authoritative. Add the swivel-chair cost of switching between them all day, and the team spends its energy reconciling systems instead of working accounts.

Context lost at handoff

The detail that makes a placement stick never makes it down the line

An account manager works through dozens of intake questions on a client call while typing notes on the side. Something always slips. By the time the role reaches a recruiter, the subtle signals — culture, the real must-haves, what the client actually meant — have already faded.

Running on spreadsheets

The revenue picture lives in a file someone has to remember to update

Lead conversion in a spreadsheet, pipeline in someone's head, renewals on whoever's calendar. It holds together until volume climbs or a person leaves — and it can't be reported on, forecast from, or handed to AI.

These are common challenges we hear from staffing and workforce businesses. The proof is in the pudding — see the results from firms that now run on Salesforce.

To be clear — this isn't an ATS replacement. Salesforce doesn't have a native applicant tracking product, so Ceipal isn't something we'd be displacing or asking you to rip out.
